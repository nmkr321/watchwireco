# Lessons from the approver
The editor reads this before every post. Written automatically from rejections; newest last.

- [LISTICLE] Visually review every rendered slide before finishing; fix any photo, model or text mismatch.
- [LISTICLE] Before finishing, fill every research handoff field and rerun validate until it passes.
- [WOULD YOU RATHER] Keep product names to two lines in side-by-side columns; keep footnotes legible with clear space above the footer.
- [CELEBRITY] Only feature real, named people wearing watches; fictional characters cannot anchor a post. Choose a real person whose wrist photo is listed in permissions.json.
- [CELEBRITY] Before choosing a story, confirm its watch or person has a rights-cleared photo in permissions.json; if none exists, pick a story that does.
- [CELEBRITY] Support every slide claim with a named source in research; delete vague generalisations such as "often costs more" instead of stating them as fact.
- [CELEBRITY] Include a dedicated, rights-cleared watch photo slide with the watch clearly visible, plus a new fact in its headline.
- [CELEBRITY] Use one reference per post: the slide photo, reference number and price must all be that exact model, verified against the brand page.
- [CELEBRITY] Say a person wears or endorses a watch only when the brand's own page states it; otherwise write 'the brand's campaign features'.
- [CELEBRITY] Keep every slide's text, including large prices, inside the side margins; shrink the figure until it clears them before rendering.
- [CELEBRITY] Check headlines and captions against the brand page too: any wear, ambassador or campaign wording needs the page's own words.
- [CELEBRITY] Give each slide's small label and headline different wording; a headline must add a new fact, not restate its label.
- [CELEBRITY] Choose a dated news event before the watch; polls and prices on angles without news value earn few saves.
- [CELEBRITY] Name a watch model only when the brand's own page, read in this run, confirms it; secondary press or retailer sources don't count.
- [CELEBRITY] If the brand page can't be read, pick a different story whose model the brand page confirms; never guess from press coverage.
- [CELEBRITY] If the lead story fails any wording, rights or model check, research the next candidate instead of forcing the lead through with softened wording.
- [CELEBRITY] If the brand page won't load, try other brand-owned pages, such as its newsroom or press release, before dropping the story.
- [CELEBRITY] Never end a run with only candidate-story notes; keep researching until one candidate passes every check, then build its carousel.
- [CELEBRITY] Confirm the news event itself on the brand's own newsroom or product page; if only press reprints show it, research the next candidate.
- [CELEBRITY] Record the brand-page URL and read date beside each model and ambassador claim in research, so the approver can verify it quickly.
