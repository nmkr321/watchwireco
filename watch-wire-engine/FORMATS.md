# Watch Wire formats (short brief for the editor and approver)

Short version of the brand guide and playbook. The full brand guide is
`The-Watch-Wire-Brand-Guide.md`; this brief must match it.

**Voice:** confident, insider, concise and checked. "Swiss watch exports rose 9.1% in
October. Fifth month in a row." Never hype, emoji headlines or "buy now".

**Value test (every post):** why would someone save, share or comment? It must be useful (a clear use or buyer: "watches for a suit", "first luxury watch under $5K", "travel GMTs"), timely (news, an auction, a release; seasonal angles only occasionally) or interactive (pick, vote, build). Lists and collections need a niche: one use case and a tight price band (roughly within 2x, e.g. $3K–$6K), never random watches from $9K to $82K.

**Always:** every number and price sourced and dated, but sources and dates live only in the Source line at the bottom of the caption, never on slides; one idea per slide; a topic tag on the cover; "spotted
wearing" unless ownership is confirmed; say whether auction prices include fees.
**Never:** price predictions, investment advice, clickbait the slides don't pay
off, reposting other creators' slides or photos, a press photo presented as ours.
**CTA:** calm and direct, one action ("Vote in the comments." / "Follow @watchwireco
for daily watch news.").

**Plain words (owner 2026-11-26: "our posts are often super hard to read").** Write like a top watch account (@luxurywatchmedia): confident and clear, using real watch words a watch fan knows (bezel, bracelet, centre links, dial, case, movement, titanium, bulk metallic glass, smoked dial, pink gold). Never baby talk or vague paraphrase ("the shiny parts", "frozen in a jumble", "winds itself" → "automatic") — owner 2026-11-27: "this is a watch page."
- Every slide reads as one full, normal sentence or phrase that makes sense on its own. No riddles or clipped fragments ("Times a stretch, not seconds" → "It measures speed, not time").
- Slide text never carries reference numbers, calibre codes, specs codes, sources or dates. "Omega Speedmaster, $7,800" — not "Ref 310.30.42.50.01.001 Speedmaster: USD 7,800, per The 1916 Company, 22 Nov 2026". The reference, source and date go in the caption.
- Name watches the way people say them: "Rolex Daytona", "Patek Nautilus", "Cartier Tank".
- Spec-sheet shorthand and codes stay off slides (vph, Hz, calibre numbers, lug-to-lug, reference numbers). Normal watch words stay; a rare one (tachymeter, tourbillon) gets a few words of explanation. At most one number per slide.
- Body ≤15 words, one sentence. Spec tables only for NEW RELEASES, 3 rows max, plain labels ("Size", "Price", "How many").
- State facts plainly, as statements: never "per GQ", "according to", "reportedly", "as reported in". Sources live only in the caption's bottom "Source: …" line, never on slides.
- Caption: line 1 is the hook in plain English (no reference numbers); then 2–4 short sentences; the question; then "Source: …" and credits at the end.

**One story, not a list of facts (owner 2026-11-27: "the words make literally zero sense").** Before writing slides, write the post as ONE short paragraph a friend could follow (the cover's promise → the answer → why it's interesting → the payoff). Then cut that paragraph into slides, in order.
- The cover makes a promise or asks a question. Slide 2 pays it off straight away (cover "Metal That Acts Like Glass" → slide 2 "The shiny parts are metal glass"), never jumps to another topic.
- Each slide follows from the one before it, like the next sentence; its label says how it connects ("Look closer", "Why glass?", "And the colour", "The twist").
- Never repeat the cover's words on a later slide, never a slide that only names a topic ("The dial", "The size").
- Read the slide headlines and bodies in order, out loud, as one paragraph. If it doesn't read as a story, rewrite it.

**Covers (owner 2026-11-26, from @luxurywatchmedia / @liveforwatches).** One huge picture fills about 70% of the cover: a face, a wrist or the watch, cropped tight. Headline = a 3–5 word twist that makes people curious, not a description: "No Snow, No Watch", "Khamzat Wears A Smile", "Left Or Right?", "Built For A Trench War" (not "What A Tachymeter Bezel Actually Does", not "Cartier's Tank Came From War"). Under it, one short fact line (watch name · one number). The tag pill above the headline. The facts and the explaining go on slide 2+.

**What gets reach:** today's news the same day; pop culture (games, films, fighters, footballers, rappers, musicians wearing watches); jaw-dropping pieces (a record price, a meteorite dial, 270 rubies); and a choice for the reader. Explainers are the backup, not the main menu.

Prices: current US retail from the brand or an official retailer, dated ("US retail, Nov 2026"); never grey-market prices as retail.
Photos: from `watch-wire-engine/src/photo.js` only (see the editor). Every photo slide carries its `credit`.

Never post the same format twice in a row; the next post must be a different type.

Template A for everything except STORY/HISTORY and STORY BEHIND, which use D. Never mix them.
Owner's direction (2026-11-25): celebrities and events first, then watches explained, new releases and new lines, features when relevant. Covers are photo-led: a full-bleed person, wrist or event photo, or several watches together; never words on a black screen.
History works only with a tension hook. A date plus a fact on its own flops.
